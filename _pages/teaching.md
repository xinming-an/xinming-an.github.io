As a statistician, teaching is not limited to formal classrooms. When collaborating with applied
researchers, I explain statistical concepts and interpret results using plain language rather than
equations. I try to make statistical ideas easier to understand by drawing on a range of teaching
strategies. My teaching philosophy is that statistics education should help learners build modeling,
programming, and communication skills while also helping them avoid misusing analytic methods,
overstating conclusions, or being misled by others.

I also believe the same statistical topics should be taught differently to different audiences based
on their interests and goals. For Ph.D. students interested in developing new statistical methods,
teaching should emphasize the mathematical and technical details. For master's students interested in
applying statistical models to real research questions, teaching should emphasize hands-on analysis,
communication, and project management. For clinical or applied collaborators, teaching should focus
more on high-level method selection and interpretation of results.

Since joining UNC in 2018, I have spent a substantial amount of time mentoring biostatisticians and
collaborators across research projects. I have co-mentored Ph.D. students on dissertations, guided
conference abstracts and posters, and helped trainees build practical programming and analysis
skills by working on real projects. From 2021 to 2023, I also provided a short course for the
Castillo Scholars program at UNC on study aims and the statistical methods best suited to address
them.
